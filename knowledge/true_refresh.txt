Specific conversion boundaries established so far:

STAGE 81130 computes floating animation time at 72FF84 from its integer
counter 72FF80, calls 7F730, an optional callback at 72FF88, and 7FB90.
It conditionally passes the floating time to 80A60 at 81232, then increments
the integer counter at 81243. Movie/replay branches compute that time
differently. Increasing the integer counter at display frequency would speed
stage animation. Sampling a floating stage time is a separate operation from
advancing the original discrete counter and invoking its stateful callbacks.

BALL 8C330 loops over eight records with stride 1B0, using original status
and handler tables. It dispatches through 557470 at 8C406 and also performs
motion, transform, radius/rotation and countdown work. It calls 8B9D0/8AEA0
under flag guards at 8C49E/8C4C8 and decrements a record countdown at 8C71F.
Its mixed responsibilities preclude treating the whole routine as render-only
or multiplying every addition/decrement by a timestep. The ball array starts
at 738DF0; 739BB4 is its selected-ball pointer in this routine.

APE 9DAB0 copies each active ball position into its character object at +258,
then calls the character-specific callback at +5C or default 9D9A0. Character
state, animation advancement and pose sampling need a verified split; changing
stage time alone cannot make monkey animation or ball motion continuous.

CAMERA B74C0 dispatches through 56A098/56A090 and constructs view transforms.
WORLD, STOBJ, EFFECT, SPRITE and BACKGROUND have their own updates/callbacks;
their time-dependent arithmetic is not controlled by the frame-wrapper wait.
They have no original common timestep; conversion uses verified operation and
callback boundaries rather than altering every counter or arithmetic operation.

Queue/lifetime distinction: 1FD80 walks linked draw records, calls record[0]
with record[4] and a zero argument, and advances through record[8]. 6A1E0's
6A2AE/6A2B6 decrement consumes queued entries counted by 7196E8, which is
reset to zero at 6A2BE. The previous research incorrectly described that count
as an effect lifetime. Neither operation should be time-scaled as a timer.

Implemented boundaries:

14F00 starts a native scene step. The original full scene path runs at the
selected rendering rate; the 2A640 wrapper uses a separate rendering deadline.
Rates below 60 retain 60 scene/physics updates and present fewer fresh frames.
Unlimited removes the rendering deadline. Elapsed-time debt is retained and
individual physics steps are bounded to one original 1/60-second unit.

Rule/input/resource/save calls in 14F00, the logical counters at 15110/1511B,
and legacy replay, sound, effect, item, sprite and character controllers keep
60 Hz timing. Their drawing callbacks and following transforms remain in the
per-scene path. 750D0 explicitly separates sprite updates from 73400 drawing.
Window input commands consume each original input edge once. Automation and
capture/performance start points use logical ticks; sample counts use scene
frames. --frames still counts rendered game scenes.

8A2C0 and the main-game 8FE80/8FC70 pipeline integrate elapsed-time force and
position. Constant-force integration composes to the original semi-implicit
Euler step at 60 Hz. Contact drag at 8B42D uses exponential time scaling;
grounded tangential force, position and drag use the fractional iterate of
p+=v+a; v=(v+a)*(1-loss). New impacts retain the original impulse response.
Previous contact flags are at ball+128, proven by 8C382..8C39F. ball+98/+9C
are the two halves of the current 64-bit flags, not current/previous flags.
Moving-stage velocity corrections in 7B320 convert frame displacement back
to velocity in original 1/60-second units. All these lowering sites verify
their exact original instruction bytes before generating native C++.

811A8 supplies continuous stage animation time without changing its integer
rule counter. 97D37 scales animation advancement before original wrap/end
logic; stored animation speed is unchanged. 9C58B uses the same interval in
its end prediction. Pose sampling remains original NJS code, executed for
the new scene. World response gains and B3BA0 camera response/seconds are
normalized; other camera controllers keep their original rule cadence and
their subsequent view transforms still run for each scene. The eleven
proved actor callbacks at 9DAFC share the continuously sampled pose path;
their separate state/facing controllers retain the original cadence.

Both goal queries still execute original swept-rectangle code. The primary
12F30 query receives ECX=ball and three caller-cleaned output pointers for
goal ID, animation group and byte-sized type (13D66..13D82, 1305D..1308E).
The distinct 13180 secondary query uses ECX and one goal-ID output pointer
(13CE3/13CEB, 1318F, 132BB..132D7). Both have separate pending-event slots.
A focused native check exercises both actual query ABIs, output widths,
receiver preservation and positive/negative swept-crossing cases. INFO 13C80
checks crossings on intermediate physics steps and latches a hit until its
next rule update. This avoids missing a zero-width trigger between rule
ticks, including goals in moving animation groups. A crossing is delivered
once and discarded on scene changes or player lifecycle changes.

Rendering publishes and presents the scene just drawn. Normal scheduler
polls do not present a retained 60 Hz picture. Original queues are rebuilt
or walked each scene, rather than retaining opaque transient command data.
The dynamic vertex/index rings avoid repeated DISCARD stalls and preserve
each draw's allocation. Texture/render-target caches remain bounded. Video
keeps its source cadence; audio mixing, device samples and native I/O clocks
remain independent. No generated frames or pose blending are introduced.

Validation is through native CPU checks, actual hidden/muted game runs,
